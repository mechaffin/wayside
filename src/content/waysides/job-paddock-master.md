You sit at the paddock with the running order in front of you and call dogs up
so they reach the start line in the right sequence. It is the job that keeps
the day moving.

## What you actually do

Call the next few dogs by number, out loud, a few slots ahead so handlers have
time to get their dog ready. Keep track of who has come up and who has not.
When someone is missing, tell the start line rather than holding everything.

You work alongside whoever is doing inspection, since every dog passes both of
you before it runs.

## What helps

A voice that carries. The paddock is outdoors, there are dogs, and there is a
lure motor running. Being heard is most of the job.

Being comfortable telling people what to do, kindly. Handlers are nervous and
some of them will drift off at exactly the wrong moment.

## What you don't need

Any prior experience. Nobody expects you to know the sport. If you can read a
list and count, you can do this.

## What it's like

You can do most of it sitting down. You are close to the field, so you see
every dog run, which is more than most volunteers get.

It is steady work rather than hard work. There is no point in the day where
you are idle, and no point where you are scrambling either.

## If you want it

Tell the club before the trial rather than on the day. The running order gets
built the morning of, and the person doing this job needs to be there when it
does.
