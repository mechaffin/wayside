Your dog stopped, sniffed the ground, or watched the bag go by. It happens
often enough that nobody at the start line is surprised, and it says almost
nothing about your dog.

## Why it happens

A plastic bag on a string is a strange thing to ask a dog to care about. Some
dogs need to see it move a few times before it registers as prey. Others are
too busy reading a field full of unfamiliar dogs to notice it at all. Young
dogs and first-timers are the two most common cases.

Heat, a full stomach, a bitch in season nearby, and a handler who is visibly
tense will all flatten a dog's interest.

## What to do right now

Take your dog off the field calmly. Do not correct them and do not repeat the
attempt on the spot. Both teach your dog that the start line is where things
get tense.

You get another run later the same day. Between now and then, keep your dog
quiet and away from the paddock.

## Before the second run

Let your dog watch other dogs course from a distance where they can see the
bag moving. Watching is how most dogs figure it out. Walk up with your dog
already looking at the lure rather than at you.

## Longer term

Practice with a rag or a plastic bag on a lunge whip in your own yard. Drag it
away from your dog, never toward them, and stop while they still want it.
Short sessions, ending early, build more interest than long ones.

Some dogs come around at eighteen months. Some never do. A dog with no interest
in a lure is not a defective dog, and there are plenty of sports that ask a
different question.

## Retrying

Nothing stops you from entering again at the next trial. Most dogs who
eventually title did not chase the first time they saw a lure.
