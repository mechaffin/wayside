You run the coffee and the hot water, and if you are feeling ambitious, a pot
of soup. The people working the field are outside all day in November, and
this is the job they will thank you for.

## What you actually do

Keep coffee going and the kettle full. The club owns a coffee maker and an
electric kettle, so there is nothing to buy or bring beyond what goes in them.

Anything hot and easy to eat standing up works. Soup, chili, something in a
slow cooker. It does not need to be impressive, it needs to be hot.

## Who it's for

The workers first. Paddock, inspection, the gate, the lure operator, and the
judge are all out there for hours and cannot leave to go find lunch. If there
is extra, everyone else is glad of it.

## What you don't need

Any knowledge of dogs or coursing. This is the one job at the trial where the
sport is irrelevant.

## What it's like

Warmer than every other job, which is not nothing in November. You are not
tied to the running order, so you can step away, watch a few dogs run, and come
back.

## If you want it

Tell the club ahead of the trial so nobody else buys coffee, and so someone
remembers to bring the equipment.
