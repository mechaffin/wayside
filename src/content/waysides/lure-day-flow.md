Lure coursing runs by breed stake, in braces of two or three, in an order drawn
that morning. You will run twice, hours apart, and you need to be listening for
both.

## Check in and the draw

Check in at the table when you arrive. The club draws running order and brace
partners for each stake after entries close on the day, so you do not know who
your dog runs with until the draw is posted. Go read it rather than waiting to
be told.

## Blankets

Each dog in a brace wears a colored blanket so the judge can tell them apart.
Yellow, pink, and blue, assigned in the draw. Put it on before you come up, get
it fastened snug, and check it again at the line. A blanket that slips
mid-course usually means a rerun.

## Roll call

Called out loud, a few braces ahead. Stakes run in a set order, so once your
stake starts, your run is close. Missing roll call is the most common way people
lose a run they paid for.

## The first course

Your brace comes up together, the huntmaster confirms everyone is ready, and
the run starts on "tally-ho". The judge scores each dog on several qualities at
once rather than on who finished first.

## Between courses

Hours, usually. Crate your dog in shade, get water into them, walk them to
loosen up, and keep them away from the paddock. The second course matters as
much as the first, because scores from both are what place your dog.

## The second course

Same brace, usually with blankets rotated. Same routine.

## Results

Placements and points go up after the stake finishes. The premium list says how
the club posts them, and it is also where the day's schedule and the judging
panel live.
