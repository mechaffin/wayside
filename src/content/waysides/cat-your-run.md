You walk your dog to the start line, take the collar off, and let go when the
judge says to. The whole thing takes under a minute.

## Walking up

Come up on leash with your dog facing the lure. The lure operator will twitch
the bag a few feet away so your dog sees it moving before the start. Let them
watch. That look is what the run is built on.

## The release

Take the collar and leash off, hold your dog by the chest or scruff, and wait
for the judge to ask if you are ready. Say yes when you mean it. There is no
rush, and nobody will start the lure until you answer.

Let go cleanly rather than pushing your dog forward. A shove usually makes a
dog turn around to look at you.

## The course

The bag runs a loop with turns in it, laid out with pulleys. Your dog chases
it. The course is longer for dogs over twelve inches at the shoulder than for
smaller dogs, and there is a time limit, though it is generous enough that a
dog who keeps chasing will finish inside it.

## What completing looks like

Your dog follows the lure with enthusiasm and finishes the course. That is the
whole standard. Speed does not matter. Style does not matter. A dog who runs
wide, cuts a corner, or takes a moment to reorient and then keeps going has
still passed.

## At the finish

The lure stops and the bag goes limp. Most dogs stand on it or grab it. Walk
out, collar your dog, and let them have the bag for a second before you take
them off the field. Then get water into them.

## When it does not go that way

Some dogs stop, sniff, or watch the bag leave without following it. That is
common, it is not a failure of training, and there is a second run later the
same day.
