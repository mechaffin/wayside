Three sports share a lure and a field and almost nothing else. One is pass-fail,
one is a stopwatch, and one is a judged competition for sighthounds only.

## Coursing Ability Test

A long course with turns, run one dog at a time. Pass or fail: your dog chases
with enthusiasm and finishes inside the time limit, or does not. Open to every
breed and to mixed breeds, twelve months and up.

Titles are counts of qualifying runs: CA, CAA, CAX.

## Fast CAT

A hundred-yard straight line, timed. Your dog runs from a handler at one end to
someone they know at the other, and the clock decides. There are no turns and
nothing to judge.

Points come from the time, adjusted for the dog's height, so a Corgi is not
competing against a Whippet. Titles are point totals: BCAT, DCAT, FCAT.

Also open to every breed and to mixed breeds.

## Lure coursing

A competition, not a test. Sighthound breeds only. Dogs run two or three at a
time in braces, wearing colored blankets so the judge can tell them apart, over
a course with turns designed to test more than speed.

A judge scores each dog on several qualities at once, including speed,
enthusiasm, agility, endurance, and how well the dog follows the lure. Scores
place dogs against each other. Titles start at JC and SC and run up through
Field Champion.

## Which one to start with

If your dog is not a sighthound, CAT or Fast CAT, and Fast CAT is the gentler
introduction because it is short and straight.

If your dog is a sighthound, CAT is still a reasonable first outing. Running
alone on a full course teaches a dog the game without another dog to chase
instead.

## The rules

AKC publishes regulations for each sport separately. Start at the
[AKC coursing and lure coursing pages](https://www.akc.org/sports/coursing/),
which link the current rulebook for each.
