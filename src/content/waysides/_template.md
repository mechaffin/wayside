Open by naming what is in front of the reader. They are standing at a specific
place holding a phone in bad light. First sentence orients them physically,
then the page explains. Not a definition, not a welcome, not a restatement of
the title. Under 40 words.

## Rules this file has to follow

Under 400 words total. Longer means it is two waysides, or it belongs in a
linked document.

No `#` in the body. The title comes from frontmatter and the layout renders
it. Body headings start at `##` and stop at `###`.

One photo maximum, alt text required. Author avatars do not count.

Never restate a document you do not own. Schedules, judging programs, premium
lists, and AKC regulations get linked, never retyped. That rule is what keeps
a printed code from going stale.

Links go inline, in prose, with a sentence saying what is in the document and
why you would open it. Name the owner, so tapping out to someone else's site
is never a surprise. Never use a bare URL as link text.

Write for the collection, not just the stop. A page in two collections cannot
assume the reader arrived from either, so state the sport when it matters.

Second person, contractions fine, no exclamation marks. A first-timer is
already nervous, and enthusiasm reads as pressure.

Slugs name the content, not the collection. Prefix with a sport only when the
content is genuinely sport-specific. The filename is permanent once a code is
printed.
