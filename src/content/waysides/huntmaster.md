The person standing beside you at the start line, watching the field rather
than the dogs, is the huntmaster. Nothing starts until they say so, and that is
the point of the job.

## What they are responsible for

Safety on the field and the order of the day. They confirm the course is clear,
that the previous brace is off it, that the lure operator is ready, and that
every handler in this brace is actually holding their dog. Then they start the
run.

## The call

They ask if you are ready. Answer honestly, out loud. Then they call
"tally-ho", and the lure starts.

"Tally-ho" is the release. Not a countdown, not a suggestion. You let go on
that word, not before it and not a beat after, because a brace where one dog
leaves early is unfair to the others and may be rerun.

## Why you wait

Two or three dogs are going to accelerate into the same turn. A staggered start
turns that into a collision risk and makes the judge's scoring meaningless.
Everything the huntmaster does is buying a clean, simultaneous start.

They will also stop a run in progress if a dog goes wide toward the line, if a
dog is loose on the field, or if the lure fails. When that happens, listen for
what they tell you to do rather than acting on your own.

## Before your brace

Have your blanket on and fastened, collar off, and your dog facing the lure
before you are called up. The huntmaster should not have to wait on gear.

## If something is wrong

Say it before the call, not after. A slipped blanket, a dog who is not settled,
a handler who is not ready: all of that is fixable in ten seconds at the line
and unfixable once the lure is moving.
