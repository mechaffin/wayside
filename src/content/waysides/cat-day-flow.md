Runs start in the morning and go until every entered dog has had two chances at
the course. You do not need to be here all day, but you do need to hear roll
call.

## Check in first

Find the table near the start line and give them your dog's name. They will
tell you your running order number and which of the two runs you are in. Nobody
comes looking for you, so check in before you set up.

## Roll call

The person at the table calls the running order out loud, a few dogs ahead of
time. Listen for your number rather than watching the clock. If you miss it,
tell the table right away instead of waiting, because they can usually slot you
back in.

## Between runs

The two runs are hours apart. Most people crate their dog in the shade, walk
them on leash away from the start line, and come back when the order gets
close. Bring more water than you think you need, and a bowl. A dog that just
ran a course is hot even in November.

## Where to wait

Stay out of the paddock until you are two or three dogs out. It gets crowded
and loud, and a dog that stands in it for an hour arrives at the line already
tired.

## What to bring

A crate or a shaded spot, water, a flat collar and leash, and your dog's
paperwork if you have not run here before. Chairs help. So does a coat, because
you will be standing still in the cold between runs.

## The schedule and the entry details

The premium list carries the start time, the closing date, the judge, and the
directions to the field. The Evergreen Basenji Club publishes it through
[BaRay Event Services](https://www.barayevents.com/), and it is the document to
trust if anything here disagrees with it.
