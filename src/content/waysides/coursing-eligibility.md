More dogs qualify than people expect. A Coursing Ability Test is open to any
breed and to mixed breeds, as long as your dog is at least twelve months old
and has an AKC number of some kind.

## Age

Twelve months on the day of the trial. Not eleven. This one is not negotiable
and it exists because a growing dog should not be running turns at speed.

## The number

Your dog needs to be in AKC's system. There are three ways in.

A registered purebred already is. A purebred without registration paperwork can
get a PAL number, which is AKC's route for dogs whose litter was never
registered or whose papers are gone. A mixed breed enrolls in Canine Partners,
which exists for exactly this and gets your dog into performance events.

Read [AKC's Canine Partners program](https://www.akc.org/canine-partners/) if
your dog is a mixed breed. It is the enrollment page, and the number arrives
quickly enough to enter a trial a few weeks out.

## Spayed and neutered dogs

Eligible. Coursing tests are performance events, so nothing here depends on
your dog being intact.

## What keeps a dog out

A bitch in season. A dog who is lame, injured, or visibly unwell. A dog with a
bandage or a cast. Blind and deaf dogs are not eligible either, because the
course requires following a moving lure across open ground.

The judge can also excuse a dog on the day for any of these, and that call is
theirs.

## Lure coursing is different

Lure coursing is open only to sighthound breeds, and its titles are earned in
competition against other dogs. If your dog is not a sighthound, CAT and Fast
CAT are the coursing sports available to you, and they are open to everyone.

## The current rules

AKC's [coursing ability test page](https://www.akc.org/sports/coursing-ability-test/)
carries the regulations, and eligibility is the section most likely to have
changed since this was written.
