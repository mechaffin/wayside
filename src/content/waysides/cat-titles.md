A Coursing Ability Test is pass or fail. Passing runs accumulate, and the
titles are just counts of them. Nothing here is scored against other dogs.

## What a qualifying run is

Your dog follows the lure with enthusiasm and completes the course inside the
time limit. One judge decides, on the spot, and you find out at the end of the
run.

## The three titles

**CA**, Coursing Ability, comes after three qualifying runs.

**CAA**, Coursing Ability Advanced, comes after ten.

**CAX**, Coursing Ability Excellent, comes after twenty-five. After that each
additional twenty-five adds a number: CAX2, CAX3, and on.

Qualifying runs count across trials, clubs, and years. There is no time window
to finish inside.

## Where the title goes

The suffix goes after your dog's registered name, and it stays there. AKC
records it once the club sends results in, which usually takes a couple of
weeks after the trial.

## Two runs a day

Most CAT trials offer two runs, so a single weekend with two trials can produce
four qualifying runs. That is why CA arrives faster than people expect.

## These are not the sighthound titles

JC, SC, and FC are lure coursing titles, and lure coursing is a judged
competition open only to sighthound breeds. CAT is open to every breed and to
mixed breeds, and it is pass-fail. Running CAT does not put you on the road to
a Field Championship, and it is not meant to.

## The rules themselves

The AKC's coursing ability page carries the current regulations, the course
length by dog height, and the time limits. It is the document to check before
you count on anything here, and it is the one that changes.

Read the AKC's [coursing ability test rules and information](https://www.akc.org/sports/coursing-ability-test/)
if you want the exact numbers.
